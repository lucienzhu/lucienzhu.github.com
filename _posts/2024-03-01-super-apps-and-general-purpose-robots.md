The shape of an AI product depends on the relationship it has with people. A personal assistant helps someone act across applications. A robot acts in a physical environment, where it must perceive the situation and handle the consequences of movement.

I see two promising directions: a personal super app that follows the user across devices, and a more autonomous machine that takes responsibility for tasks in the world. They place different demands on context, interfaces and reliability.

{% include article-image.html src="/img/blog/super-apps-and-general-purpose-robots/tool-and-creature.png" alt="Two directions for AI products: tools and independent creatures" width="1514" height="682" %}

## A personal super app follows the task

A task often spans several applications and devices. Preparing a trip may involve a conversation on a phone, documents on a computer and a booking service. A personal assistant would be useful if it could maintain the intention and relevant context across those transitions.

That makes continuity a product advantage. The interface can change with the device while the task, decisions and progress remain available. A small screen may suit a short confirmation; a larger workspace may suit comparing alternatives or reviewing a result.

{% include article-image.html src="/img/blog/super-apps-and-general-purpose-robots/apps-engines-and-systems.png" alt="Applications, engines, and operating systems across platforms" width="968" height="762" size="medium" %}

Cross-platform software already offers a useful design lesson: the same service can have several front ends. An AI assistant adds a harder requirement. It has to understand what should carry between contexts and which actions the user intends to delegate.

## Context creates value and obligations

A personal assistant needs to know enough about a person to be useful. Preferences, previous decisions and working material can reduce repeated explanation. That information also gives the product obligations: clear access boundaries, understandable use of context and ways to correct what it has inferred.

Trust has to be earned through behavior. A product that remembers a preference incorrectly can make repeated mistakes more efficiently. A useful assistant lets the person inspect important assumptions and change them.

The interface may also adapt to the task. The brain.ai example below illustrates the direction of generating an interface around an intention. A comparison, a plan and a completed action need different presentations; forcing them all into the same conversation format can hide information the user needs.

{% include article-image.html src="/img/blog/super-apps-and-general-purpose-robots/brain-ai.png" alt="An interface example from brain.ai" width="1200" height="627" size="medium" caption="brain.ai interface example, included in the March 2024 essay." %}

A browser is one possible workspace because it exposes information and services together. Other tasks depend on local files, devices or physical activity. The starting point should be the task and its necessary actions.

## A robot has a body to account for

A robot extends this problem into physical interaction. It must connect perception and decisions to motion, contact and changes in its surroundings. A useful task description alone does not provide reliable control.

Form should follow that work. A mobile platform, an arm and a humanoid body offer different capabilities and constraints. Reach, balance, power, maintenance and the environment all shape the design. More general perception may help a robot interpret more situations, while control and embodiment still need their own engineering and evaluation.

The goal of a general-purpose robot is attractive because useful tasks vary. Progress can begin with a bounded activity, where success can be measured and failures understood, then expand as capabilities become dependable.

Calling such a machine an “AI being” expresses an aspiration for autonomy and interaction. Product quality still comes down to what it can accomplish with the people and objects around it.

## Experience is where the system meets the user

Both directions require much more than access to a model. A personal assistant needs continuity, usable permissions and effective feedback. A robot needs those qualities alongside dependable physical behavior.

A distinctive product chooses an activity and makes the whole experience work: starting the task, expressing an intention, seeing progress, correcting mistakes and reaching a useful result. That is where model capability becomes a reason to keep using it.
